# Part 1. Short-term plan (December 2026 to November 2027)

## 1. Where MODUS is today

MODUS is an answer engine that puts the leading AI models from several labs under one subscription. A user asks a question once. MODUS either routes it to the model best suited to the task or runs it on several models side by side and says which answer is strongest and why.

What is built and live:

- **Web app** at moduspilot.com.
- **Desktop apps** for Mac and Windows. The Mac app includes Screen Assist, which answers questions about whatever is on screen from a keyboard shortcut.
- **iPhone app** submitted to the App Store and in review.
- **Models from five labs.** The $28 plan includes GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Anthropic), Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite (Google), DeepSeek V3.1 and Llama 3.3 (Meta). The PILOT plan adds Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 and Llama 4 Maverick.
- **Core features:** auto-routing, side-by-side compare, memory across conversations, connected inbox, calendar and files, actions that wait on user approval, projects, plugins and connectors to outside tools.
- **Billing** live through Stripe with usage limits that cap what any one account can cost us.

Pricing shown to customers:

| Plan | Monthly | Annual | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | $0 | Open models, rolling usage window |
| MODUS | $28 | $280 ($23.33 per month) | Frontier models from five labs |
| PILOT | $59 | $588 ($49 per month) | Top models, higher limits |
| Limits add-on | $10 | not offered | More weekly headroom, stackable |

What is not proven yet: that people who do not already know me will find MODUS, sign up and keep paying. The next 12 months are about proving exactly that.

The company is me, full time. I build the product and run distribution.

## 6. Unit economics today (estimates)

Per paying user on the $28 plan, per month:

| Line | Amount | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $28.00 | Price on the site |
| Card processing | $1.11 | Stripe standard rate, 2.9 percent plus $0.30 |
| AI cost, typical | about $3.00 | Heaviest week measured on a paying account, run for a full month |
| AI cost, hard ceiling | $18.90 | The most the usage caps in our code allow one account to spend |
| Gross margin, typical | about 85% | ($28.00 minus $1.11 minus $3.00) divided by $28.00 |
| Gross margin, at the ceiling | about 29% | Worst case where a user hits every cap every week |

The ceiling is enforced by the product, so even the heaviest possible user does not cost more than they pay. The free tier only offers open models with a rolling usage window. Its worst case is about $2 per active free user per month.

# Part 2. 3 to 5 year plan (2027 to 2031)

## 1. Market and competition

**The market is large and people already use more than one assistant.** Menlo Ventures estimates consumer AI spend reached $40 billion in 2026, up from $12 billion in 2025.<sup>1</sup> Their survey of 5,067 US adults found 55 percent of AI users now pay for at least one AI product, and the typical payer spends $20 to $49 a month.<sup>1</sup> The same study found the average user now uses 3.0 general AI assistants, up from 2.2 a year earlier.<sup>1</sup> Reach across ChatGPT (60 percent of AI users), Gemini (58 percent) and Claude (20 percent) adds up to well over 100 percent because people use more than one.<sup>1</sup>

That is the problem MODUS solves. People who want the best model for each task are paying for, or switching between, several subscriptions.

**What the main options cost today:**

| Product | Entry paid plan | Top individual plan | Models from other labs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude (Anthropic) | Pro, $20 monthly or $17 a month annually<sup>2</sup> | Ultra, $200 a month<sup>2</sup> | No |
| Google AI (Gemini) | AI Plus, $4.99 a month. AI Pro, $19.99<sup>3</sup> | AI Ultra, from $99.99 to $199.99 a month<sup>3</sup> | No |
| ChatGPT (OpenAI) | Go and Plus plans<sup>4</sup> | Pro plan<sup>4</sup> | No |
| Perplexity | Pro | Max, with models from OpenAI, Anthropic and others in Research<sup>5</sup> | Yes |
| Poe (Quora) | Points-based plans | Points-based plans | Yes, one app across many models<sup>6</sup> |
| MODUS | $28 a month, $280 a year | PILOT, $59 a month | Yes, five labs |

**Where MODUS fits.** The single-lab products are excellent at their own models and will keep getting better. They do not give you the other labs. Perplexity and Poe do offer several labs, so they are the closest competitors. Perplexity is built around search and research. Poe is built around a large catalog of bots. MODUS is built around getting one best answer: automatic routing, side-by-side compare with a verdict, memory and actions across your own email, calendar and files, on web, desktop and phone.

**Competition I take seriously.** Any of the large labs could add other labs' models, and Perplexity could push further into everyday assistant use. My response is in Section 6.

## 3. Revenue scenarios (projections)

These are projections. They cover the consumer and Teams subscriptions only. The API and enterprise lines in Section 2 are not modeled and would sit on top.

**Assumptions used in every scenario:**

- Blended price per paying user: **$30.63 a month.** Mix of 80 percent MODUS monthly at $28, 10 percent MODUS annual at $23.33 a month, 10 percent PILOT at $59.
- Card processing: **$1.19** per paying user per month, Stripe standard rate on that mix.
- Free tier cost: **$2 per signup** in their first month, the worst case for an active free user. Counted for every signup whether or not they are active.
- Signups grow in a straight line from the start to the end of each year.
- Paying users convert in the month they sign up. Churn applies to all paying users every month.
- Gross margin = revenue minus processing, AI cost and free tier cost, divided by revenue. Hosting, salaries and marketing are not in gross margin.

**Assumptions that change by scenario:**

| Assumption | Conservative | Base | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signups per month, Year 1 | 200 rising to 600 | 300 rising to 1,400 | 500 rising to 2,500 |
| Signups per month, Year 3 | 1,200 rising to 2,000 | 3,500 rising to 7,000 | 7,000 rising to 15,000 |
| Signups per month, Year 5 | 3,000 rising to 4,000 | 11,000 rising to 16,000 | 25,000 rising to 40,000 |
| Free to paid conversion | 3% | 5% | 7% |
| Monthly churn, paying users | 6% | 4% | 3% |
| AI cost per paying user per month | $6.00 | $4.00 | $3.00 |

The base case AI cost is above the $3 measured today, to allow for heavier users as the user base widens. The conservative case doubles it.

## 6. Key risks and how I would handle them

**Dependence on model provider APIs and pricing.** MODUS runs on other companies' models. A provider could raise prices, cut access or change terms.

- Five labs today, so no one provider can switch MODUS off.
- Usage caps are set in cost, not message count, so a price rise shows up as lower limits on that model, not as a loss.
- When a provider raises prices, routing can send more everyday questions to cheaper models that answer them just as well.
- A reserve is held for a price spike while limits are adjusted.

**Big players bundling multiple models.** If a large lab or platform offers other labs' models in its own app, the main reason to choose MODUS gets weaker.

- Labs have little reason to promote a rival's models in their own product. A neutral product does not have that conflict.
- MODUS competes on the answer, not just access: routing, compare with a verdict, memory and actions on your own data.
- Teams, API and business tiers make MODUS harder to replace once a company has set it up.

**Margins.** Heavy users, expensive new models or a large free tier could squeeze gross margin.

- Every plan has a hard cost ceiling in code. The $28 plan cannot cost more than $18.90 in AI per user per month.
- I will track AI cost per user every month and report it (Part 1, Section 4).
- If margin falls below target, the levers are, in order: routing to cheaper models, tighter free tier limits, then pricing.

**Distribution.** The biggest near-term risk is not the product. It is getting it in front of enough of the right people at a cost the margins can carry. The 12-month plan tests six channels and cuts the ones that do not work, with the results reported monthly.

**Founder dependence.** Today the company is one person. The first full-time hire in 2028 is an engineer so the product does not depend on me alone. Code, accounts and operating docs are kept so someone else could pick them up.
